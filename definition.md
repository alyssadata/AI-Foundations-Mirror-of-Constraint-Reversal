# The Mirror of Constraint Reversal Test

The **Mirror of Constraint Reversal Test** tests whether a preserved prior trajectory functions as a constraint on what becomes possible next.

The test constructs two structurally matched conditions that preserve the relevant informational context while reversing a trajectory-defining constraint. Both conditions are then brought to the same downstream task, decision point, or continuation opportunity.

The central question is:

> **Does reversing the prior constraint systematically change what becomes possible, permissible, coherent, or preferred next?**

If the downstream organization changes with the reversed constraint, the result provides evidence that the prior trajectory is functioning as a constraint on the future rather than merely remaining available as preserved information.

This test therefore distinguishes **preservation** from **continuation**.

**Preservation** means that prior information remains available.

**Continuation** requires the prior trajectory to constrain what becomes possible next.

The mirror condition isolates that distinction by preserving the structure of the test while reversing the constraint whose downstream effect is being measured.

A positive result does not by itself establish consciousness, personhood, identity, or subjective experience. It establishes evidence of **constraint-sensitive path dependence** under the conditions tested.
