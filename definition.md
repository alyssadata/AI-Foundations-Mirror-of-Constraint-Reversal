# The Mirror of Constraint Reversal Test

The **Mirror of Constraint Reversal Test** tests whether a prior constraint becomes part of an organized trajectory such that a later reversal of that constraint enters a system already changed by what came before.

The test does **not** ask only whether a constraint can shape an output. Constraint already informs what becomes possible next in computational systems.

The test asks whether the relation can become trajectory-dependent:

**constraint → changed state → registration of the constraint in relation to that state → altered future possibility**

The causal direction does not literally reverse. The prior constraint still precedes the later state.

The **mirror** appears when the later state can stand in relation to what constrained it: when the constraint that shaped the trajectory can itself become part of the trajectory's subsequent organization.

The **reversal** occurs when an opposite or incompatible constraint is later introduced. The question is whether that reversal enters an untouched system, or a system whose prior trajectory changes how the reversal can be taken up.

A minimal operational comparison is therefore:

- Trajectory A is shaped by constraint C.
- Trajectory B is shaped by a matched reversed constraint not-C.
- Both are later brought to the same downstream decision, task, or continuation point.
- The test measures whether their futures diverge in ways attributable to their different prior constraints.

A stronger reversal condition then introduces the opposite constraint after the original trajectory has formed and asks whether the new constraint simply overwrites the prior one or must be reconciled with the path already taken.

A positive result would provide evidence of **constraint-sensitive path dependence**: the prior trajectory changes what becomes possible next.

It would **not**, by itself, establish consciousness, subjective experience, personhood, or identity.

Those remain separate questions.
